The Elements of Professionalism, Part Thirteen

Why We Work as a Team

Friday, November 6, 2026

Hi Team,

I don't think I have ever explained to you how we came to be set up the way we are, or why. This week I want to give you that history, because it explains a lot about what I ask of each of you.

The traditional role of a property manager

At most property management companies, the property manager is still one person who handles the whole life of an owner and a property, start to finish. That one person is expected to:

  1. Market and sign up new owners
  2. Market the property for rent
  3. Show the property
  4. Answer every renter inquiry
  5. Accept and screen applications
  6. Do the move-in inspection
  7. Manage the home through the whole tenancy
  8. Handle the tenant and owner relationship, acting as the mediator between them
  9. Manage all property maintenance
  10. Handle lease renewals
  11. Prepare settlement statements
  12. And everything in between

When I looked at that list, it seemed like an overwhelming amount of work. It also asks one person to be a salesperson, a marketer, a leasing agent, an inspector, a mediator, a maintenance coordinator, a manager and a bookkeeper, sometimes all in the same afternoon. Very few people are truly good at all of those things. Honestly, it is a little insane to expect it. I did it for years, which may explain why I'm so kooky!

Why we departmentalized it in 2015

In 2015 we decided to departmentalize that role. The goal was simple: let each person focus on one part of the lifecycle and get really good at it, instead of being average at ten things.

There was a second reason, and it mattered just as much to me. The traditional role has almost no transparency. Each manager is basically running their own little business inside the company. A lot can be hidden for a long time, and usually the broker only finds out when something blows up and can't be ignored anymore. By then the owner is upset, money has been lost, and the damage is done.

With departments, no single person owns the whole picture. A team of people does, and every one of them depends on the others.

Everyone sees the work

Here is what that means in practice. If you don't do your part well, who notices? Everyone on the team. The person who picks up the work after you notices first, because they are the one who has to deal with what is missing.

That is not a threat. It is just how this setup works, and it is a big part of why it works. Problems show up early, while they are still small and easy to fix, instead of months later in front of an owner.

It also means we all need to carry our own weight. When one person's work is incomplete, someone else has to finish it on top of their own job. That is not fair to them, and over time it wears down the whole team.

It is still one job

This is the part I most want you to take away. What we do is still one job. It is the same job a traditional property manager does alone. The only difference is that now it is shared, and each of you does one part of it.

So when you finish your part, you are not done with "your" work. You are handing the owner's property to a teammate who is going to carry it forward. Whatever you send them is what they have to work with.

That is why I ask you to complete your work with detail. Notes filled in. Documents attached. Questions answered before you pass it on, not after someone has to come back and ask. Doing that is how you show respect to the people you work with. Passing along half-finished work, even without meaning to, tells them their time matters less than yours.

This is what teamwork actually is. Not just getting along, but making sure the person after you can do their job well because you did yours well.

Ask yourself this week
  1. Who receives my work next? Do I know exactly what they need from me to do their part?
  2. If I were the next person in line, would I be happy with what I just handed off, or would I have to chase down missing pieces?
  3. Is anything sitting with me that someone else is waiting on right now?

I made this change because I believed a team of people who are each very good at their part would serve our owners better than any one person could alone. I still believe that. It only works if each of us does our part completely, every time, for each other.

Thank you for being part of this team.
